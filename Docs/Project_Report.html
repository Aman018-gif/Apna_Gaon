1. Abstract

Mohalla Hero: Choices That Count is a 2D top-down, narrative-driven civic-education story game developed in Unity 6 using C#. The project addresses the widespread deficit of accessible, engaging civic education for school-age and college-age audiences in urban India by embedding pedagogically sound lessons inside a compelling, choice-driven game narrative. Players assume the role of a newcomer to Shanti Nagar, Ward 7 — a fictional Indian city neighbourhood — and navigate six thematic chapters covering electricity and water conservation, anti-bullying behaviour, honest marketplace conduct, evidence-based civic advocacy, peaceful protest and petition, and democratic electoral participation.

Each gameplay decision is evaluated against four karma statistics — Civic Sense, Green Score, Courage, and Honesty — and is followed by a "Why it Matters" explanatory card that articulates the real-world implication of every choice. A resident trust system determines which of twelve neighbourhood characters support the player's protest march, while a composite Mohalla Rating formula aggregates karma share, supporter share, and informed-voter share to determine one of four distinct narrative endings. The game world reacts dynamically: potholed, dimly lit streets at low ratings transform into festival-lit streets with murals at high ones, providing visible, environmental feedback on civic progress.

The technical architecture is deliberately code-first: all sprites are drawn procedurally via SpriteFactory, all UI is constructed at runtime via UIFactory, and the branching story is authored in the Ink scripting language and run by a custom lightweight interpreter, InkLite, built specifically for this project to eliminate all external package dependencies. The game targets both desktop (keyboard) and Android/iOS mobile (touch controls) platforms and includes a comprehensive automated test suite of 31 EditMode and 1 PlayMode tests covering story correctness, world reachability, and all gameplay systems.

This report documents the theoretical background, system architecture, key design decisions, the implementation progress as of the mid-semester evaluation, user-interface design rationale, and a detailed plan for the work remaining before the final submission.

Keywords: Civic Education, Serious Games, Unity 6, C#, Ink Scripting Language, Narrative Game Design, Karma System, Mobile Game Development, India, Procedural Art Generation, Game-Based Learning, Hinglish, Democratic Participation.

2. Introduction

2.1 Background and Motivation

India is the world's largest democracy, yet civic participation among young urban citizens remains structurally under-supported. Traditional civic education — delivered through textbooks, lectures, and occasional awareness campaigns — has consistently failed to create lasting attitudinal change. Studies in educational psychology demonstrate that experiential, choice-based learning produces significantly deeper retention and behavioural transfer than passive instruction (Kolb, 1984; Mayer, 2019). Game-Based Learning (GBL) leverages intrinsic motivation, immediate feedback loops, and narrative immersion to place learners in simulated environments where the consequences of civic decisions become personally meaningful without real-world risk.

Mohalla Hero: Choices That Count was conceived to fill this gap. Designed for players aged 12 and above, it uses the universally familiar framework of a story game to explore six core civic themes: resource conservation, safe and respectful peer interaction, marketplace honesty, evidence-based protest, peaceful civic action, and informed electoral participation. These themes were mapped directly to India's National Curriculum Framework objectives and to the ten Fundamental Duties enumerated in Article 51A of the Indian Constitution, ensuring academic credibility and national curricular alignment.

The project is a direct evolution of an earlier B.Tech capstone, Apna Gaon — a village-simulation game built on the same Unity codebase. Mohalla Hero reuses the proven core engine infrastructure (procedural pixel art, tile-map world building, A* NPC pathfinding, and OS-native text-to-speech voices) while replacing all village-specific gameplay systems with the urban civic story systems described throughout this report.

2.2 Project Objectives

  • Design a six-chapter branching narrative that teaches measurable civic values through consequential, cross-chapter player choices.
  • Implement a robust karma-and-trust feedback system that gives players unambiguous, explained signals about the impact of every decision.
  • Build a fully code-generated game world requiring no pre-made art assets, deliverable as a zero-dependency Unity project.
  • Support both desktop keyboard and mobile touch platforms from a single codebase using a unified virtual-input abstraction layer.
  • Maintain a comprehensive automated test suite that validates story correctness, world reachability, and all system logic.
  • Ensure all content is safe, inclusive, age-appropriate, and free from references to real political entities.

2.3 Scope and Deliverables

The project scope encompasses: (1) a fully playable Unity 6 game project; (2) a six-chapter story authored in the Ink scripting language; (3) a custom Ink interpreter (InkLite); (4) all core gameplay systems (karma, trust, mission, protest, election, save/load); (5) a procedurally generated 96 × 64-tile city world; (6) all UI screens built programmatically; (7) a 32-test automated test suite; and (8) the complete documentation set comprising this report, a design-decisions record, a UI layout plan, a beginner setup guide, and a free-asset list.

Multiplayer, cloud saves, server-side analytics, and real-money monetisation are explicitly out of scope for this semester's deliverable.

3. Problem Statement

3.1 Civic Education Gap in Urban India

Despite India's constitutional mandate for civic awareness, the existing educational infrastructure largely limits civic instruction to rote-learning of constitutional provisions and government structure. Translating abstract constitutional concepts into daily behaviour — turning off unused electrical equipment, refusing bribes, challenging misinformation, exercising the Right to Information, participating in peaceful protests, and casting an informed vote — remains a persistent, unresolved pedagogical challenge across the Indian education system.

The problem is especially acute among urban youth between 12 and 25 years of age. Rapid urbanisation has created dense, anonymous neighbourhoods where communal accountability is eroding. Young residents frequently lack both the vocabulary and the confidence to act as civic agents in their own mohallas. Survey data from NGOs operating in urban civic education consistently report that knowledge alone does not predict behaviour; young people need emotional engagement and practised decision-making to convert awareness into action.

India's electoral literacy programmes (SVEEP, ELC) and environmental awareness campaigns reach millions but rely on passive media — posters, videos, and workshops — that lack the interactive, consequence-bearing structure necessary for genuine attitude change. The Election Commission of India has itself noted that first-time voter turnout remains significantly below the national average in urban constituencies, precisely where civic indifference is most pronounced.

3.2 Deficiencies in Existing Digital Solutions

Existing digital civic education products fall into two broad, inadequate categories:

  • Static educational apps: Quiz-format or slideshow apps (e.g., government voter-awareness portals, iGOT civic modules) provide factual information without emotional or narrative engagement. Completion rates are low and knowledge retention is minimal beyond the short term because there are no consequences attached to correct or incorrect answers.
  • Commercial games with superficial civic themes: Mainstream governance or city-building games (e.g., Cities: Skylines) incorporate civic mechanics without grounding decisions in Indian socio-cultural context, local language, or calibrated moral consequence. These also require hardware and purchase budgets inaccessible to the target demographic.

The core problem is therefore threefold: (1) the absence of a culturally contextualised, Hinglish-language narrative civic game; (2) the lack of a system that delivers immediate, explained feedback on the moral consequence of every choice within an immersive narrative; and (3) the unavailability of such a product as a free, zero-asset, deployable package suitable for school or college computer labs and personal Android devices.

Design Response: Mohalla Hero directly addresses all three gaps with a Hinglish narrative set in a culturally authentic fictional Indian neighbourhood, a post-choice "Why it Matters" card system that explains every significant decision, and a fully procedural, asset-free Unity 6 project that runs on any machine with Unity 6 installed and builds to Android without paid licence modules.

4. Methodological and Theoretical Background

4.1 Theoretical Framework

4.1.1 Game-Based Learning (GBL) and Experiential Learning

The project is grounded in Kolb's (1984) Experiential Learning Cycle, which posits that durable learning requires a sequence of concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation, and active experimentation. In Mohalla Hero, each story mission provides a concrete experience (a civic dilemma in a familiar Indian neighbourhood setting); the "Why it Matters" card enables reflective observation through real-world explanation; the karma-stat feedback system operationalises abstract conceptualisation by showing the player how values accumulate over time; and the player's next choice constitutes active experimentation — completing the full cycle within a few minutes of play.

Mayer's (2019) meta-analysis of 216 studies on game-based learning found that games with immediate outcome feedback, adaptive difficulty, and narrative framing consistently outperformed passive instruction in knowledge acquisition and attitudinal change. Mohalla Hero implements all three characteristics: the "Why it Matters" card delivers immediate outcome feedback; the story branches adapt to accumulated karma and trust; and the narrative frame of a new resident finding their place in Shanti Nagar provides strong contextual motivation.

4.1.2 Narrative Persuasion Theory

Green and Brock's (2000) Transportation-Imagery Model demonstrates that narrative immersion ("transportation") suppresses counterarguing and increases susceptibility to attitude change. By casting the player as a newcomer who genuinely cares about the neighbourhood's wellbeing, the game leverages transportation to make civic lessons feel personally relevant rather than externally imposed. The deliberate fictional framing — Shanti Nagar, Ward 7, fictional candidates with fictional party symbols — creates psychological safety for moral exploration without partisan pressure, making the game equally accessible across all political backgrounds.

4.1.3 Consequential Branching and Moral Agency

Choices in Mohalla Hero carry deliberate cross-chapter consequences. Returning Lala-ji's extra ₹100 in Chapter 3 raises his trust, causing him to hand over crucial evidence in Chapter 4 rather than making the player search for it. How the player stopped the Chapter 2 bullying incident changes available dialogue options in Chapter 4. Accepting Netaji's bribe in Chapter 4 costs Honesty and Civic karma that is difficult to recover. This design principle, derived from Murray (1997) and Swink (2009), ensures players develop a genuine sense of moral agency — the understanding that past actions define future opportunities — mirroring real civic life where reputation and relationships compound over time.

4.2 Engine Architecture

The game engine is Unity 6 (version 6000.6.3f1, LTS), the industry standard for cross-platform 2D and 3D real-time development. Unity's component-entity model, integrated physics engine, and Universal Render Pipeline (URP) provide a mature, well-documented platform for 2D tile-based games. Key Unity subsystems utilised are:

  • uGUI — Unity's legacy UI system, chosen for runtime programmatic construction, full touch support, and fine-grained rendering-order control. All UI elements are instantiated from code via UIFactory, eliminating scene prefab dependency entirely.
  • Tilemaps — Unity's built-in Tilemap system powers the 96 × 64-tile city grid, constructed procedurally at runtime by WorldBuilder.cs, eliminating the need for external tile assets.
  • ScriptableObjects — used for KarmaStatAsset instances (Civic Sense, Green Score, Courage, Honesty), encapsulating per-stat configuration (name, Hindi name, colour, description, XP caps, rank titles). The system functions correctly even without these assets, falling back to coded defaults.
  • TextAssets — Ink story files carry the .txt extension so that Unity's asset pipeline loads them as raw text, eliminating the need for the Ink Compiler package.

4.3 Execution Model

4.3.1 Ink Scripting Language

All narrative content is authored in Ink, an open-source scripting language designed for interactive fiction by Inkle Studios and used in commercial titles including 80 Days, Heaven's Vault, and Sable. Ink provides a clean syntax for branching dialogue, conditional logic, variable tracking, and tagged metadata — all essential for a civic story with consequential branches and systemic feedback. The Mohalla Hero story spans seven files: mohalla.txt (master file: variables, intro, character routing, world objects) and six chapter files (m1_bijli_bachao.txt through m6_election_mela.txt), linked via Ink's INCLUDE directive.

4.3.2 InkLite — Custom Ink Interpreter

Rather than relying on the official ink-unity-integration package (which requires package installation and a compilation step), the project ships a built-in interpreter, InkLite (Scripts/Story/InkLite.cs), a tested 450-line custom parser and runner. InkLite implements a sufficient subset of the Ink specification: knots and diverts, once-only (*) and sticky (+) choices, conditional branches and gathers, tags, VAR declarations and ~ assignments, inline conditionals {cond: a|b}, and shuffle sequences {~shuffle}. Because the story files are valid standard Ink, migration to the official runtime requires only swapping the interpreter class — a migration path fully documented in the setup guide.

4.3.3 Game Loop and Session Boot

Unity's [RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod] attribute on GameManager.Boot() triggers initialization in any scene, making build order irrelevant. The 14-step boot sequence guarantees all data dependencies are satisfied before any world or AI logic runs: pure systems first (SaveSystem, GameEvents, Balance, KarmaSystem, TrustSystem), then world construction (WorldMap, TimeManager, WorldBuilder, AreaManager, NavGrid), then agents (PlayerController, ProtestManager, ElectionManager, NPCManager, CityVisuals), and finally UI (UIManager, HudScreen, Main Menu or session resume). This ordered initialization eliminates the entire class of null-reference exceptions common in complex Unity projects.

4.4 Security Considerations

4.4.1 Save-File Integrity

The save file (mohallahero_save.json) stores only story variables that differ from their coded defaults — a differential approach that keeps files small and human-readable. This minimal-data design limits the attack surface for manual save manipulation. While no cryptographic checksum is applied in this iteration, the architecture is designed so that one can be added by wrapping SaveSystem.Write() without modifying any other system. The save triggers automatically on chapter completion, rest, F5, and app-to-background on mobile.

4.4.2 Content Safety

All characters, party names, party symbols, and party colours are entirely fictional. No real politicians, political parties, or real party symbols appear anywhere in the code, assets, or story files — a hard constraint enforced both by project policy and by EditMode tests. All dialogue is reviewed to be appropriate for players aged 12 and above, consistent with CBFC U/A guidelines. The SpriteFactory.Finger sprite was explicitly redrawn to remove any ambiguous gesture.

4.4.3 Input Validation and Anti-Cheat

Player input is routed through GameInput.cs, which normalises keyboard and touch events into a unified virtual-key model. Input guards — UIManager.JustOpened, ClosedThisFrame, and per-screen frame guards — prevent double-invocation of choices, a common bug that can cause unintended "soft cheating" (skipping a dialogue page before reading it). The Escape key can exit a conversation only before the first choice is presented, preventing mid-decision bail-out. The Slogan mini-game's violent submissions are refused with a penalty rather than accepted and penalised silently, giving the player explicit feedback.

5. Architecture and Key Design Decisions

5.1 Code-First Philosophy

The single most consequential architectural decision in Mohalla Hero is that everything is built from code. There are no prefabs, no sprite atlases, no animation controller assets, and no scene-authored UI hierarchies. This philosophy delivers several concrete benefits:

  • Zero-asset distribution: The Unity project can be cloned and run without any additional download step. Critical for college-lab deployment where internet access may be unreliable or restricted.
  • Deterministic tests: Because every game object is instantiated programmatically, the EditMode test suite can construct the full game state without Unity's Play Mode, enabling fast, headless batch execution on CI or in a terminal.
  • Single source of truth: All world positions are declared in WorldLayout.cs, all tunable numbers in Balance.cs, all colour tokens in UIFactory.Theme, and all NPC data in NpcDatabase.cs. Changing a value propagates everywhere automatically.
  • Platform portability: The project opens identically on Windows, macOS, and Linux Unity installations and builds to Android and iOS without platform-specific asset variants or conditional compilation paths.

5.2 Custom Ink Runtime (InkLite)

The decision to author a custom Ink interpreter was driven by three factors: (1) the need for zero external dependencies; (2) the requirement that the story run from plain .txt TextAssets without a compiler step; and (3) the educational value of the interpreter as a software engineering artefact demonstrating parser design and interpreter pattern implementation. InkLite is fully unit-tested: the EditMode suite runs two complete story playthroughs through the real Ink files, asserting that the best-choices path reaches the Mohalla Hero! ending and that a worst-choices path still finishes without soft-locks and with a measurably lower rating. The interpreter correctly handles all story constructs present in the six chapter files.

5.3 State Management

All game state is centralised in StoryState.cs, which maintains a dictionary of every story variable declared with VAR in the Ink files. The save system serialises only entries that differ from their coded defaults — a differential-save approach that keeps save files small (typically under 2 KB) and human-readable while guaranteeing correct restoration on load. Mission progress is derived lazily from story variables: MissionSystem reads doneVar booleans to determine which chapter is current, rather than maintaining its own progress counters. The trust system stores per-resident trust as trust_<id> variables directly in StoryState, keeping all state co-located and consistently serialisable without separate persistence logic.

5.4 Karma and Rating Formula

The Mohalla Rating is the game's primary win-condition signal and is computed by EndingEvaluator.cs using the following formula:

Mohalla Rating = (70 × average karma share) + (10 × supporter share) + (20 × informed-voter share) − (25 × violence count)

Where karma share for each stat = earned XP ÷ cap (Civic cap: 150, Green: 75, Courage: 90, Honesty: 75). Average taken across four stats.

The ward election winner, and therefore the narrative ending, is determined by the Mohalla Rating and informed-voter count — never by the player's own vote (which is secret and never judged). This design teaches that informing others matters more than any single vote, without discouraging participation.

7. Work Completed Till Mid-Semester

7.1 Research and Design Phase

The research and design phase occupied the first four weeks of the semester and produced all foundational documents and decisions before game-specific implementation began.

7.1.1 Literature Review

A systematic review of Game-Based Learning literature was conducted, covering Mayer's (2019) meta-analysis of 216 educational game studies, Squire's (2011) work on digital games in civic education, and Gee's (2003) principles of effective video game learning. Additionally, the team reviewed existing Indian civic education digital products (iGOT, MyGov Learning, ELC materials, SVEEP portals) to catalogue their design limitations. The review concluded that the most effective design combination for the target audience was: a first-person narrative perspective with the player as protagonist, Hinglish dialogue that mixes Hindi and English naturally, immediate consequence feedback on every choice, and a visible neighbourhood reputation metric that accumulates across all six chapters.

7.1.2 Civic Theme Selection and Curriculum Mapping

Six civic themes were selected through a faculty-guided consultation and mapped to: (a) the Indian Constitution's Fundamental Duties (Article 51A), (b) the Right to Information Act 2005, (c) NCERT Social Science curriculum objectives for Classes 8–10, and (d) ECI SVEEP programme objectives. This mapping established the academic credibility of the game's content and its suitability for use in school and college civic education contexts.

7.1.3 Narrative Design

The six-chapter story arc was designed collaboratively, with each chapter assigned a primary NPC guide. Cross-chapter consequences were mapped on a full decision tree, including: the Lala-ji money return (Chapter 3 → Chapter 4 evidence path), the bullying intervention method (Chapter 2 → Chapter 4 Bunty dialogue), the doctored photo choice (Chapter 4 trust impact), and the bribe-refusal / acceptance / reporting trident (Chapter 4). All fictional character names, party names (Rocket, See-saw, Lantern), and party colours were verified against official ECI records to confirm no resemblance to real entities.

7.1.4 World Design and Map Layout

The 96 × 64-tile world map was designed as a believable Ward with distinct zones: a top half containing the school, Gulmohar Park (with its broken swings, renovation board, pond, and hedge), the Ward Office, Seva Clinic, and a row of voter homes; a bottom half containing the walled Bazaar Gali (unlocked in Chapter 3), party offices with a raddi pile, the Community Hall, and the walled Mela Maidan (unlocked after the petition). Every named location serves at least one mission purpose, and the map was validated by automated flood-fill reachability tests.

7.1.5 Design Document Production

The following design documents were produced and are maintained in the Docs/ folder:

  • DESIGN_DECISIONS.md — all architectural and narrative decisions with full rationale
  • UI_LAYOUT_PLAN.md — screen-by-screen ASCII wireframes, colour palette, text sizes
  • SETUP_GUIDE.md — step-by-step setup for non-Unity users, build instructions, and story-editing guide
  • ASSET_LIST.md — enumerated free replacement asset packs (OpenGameArt, Google Fonts) for the procedural art

7.2 Implementation Part

Implementation began in Week 5. As of the mid-semester checkpoint, the following modules are fully implemented and all 32 tests are passing.

7.2.1 Infrastructure Layer

  • GameManager.cs — application lifecycle, 14-step boot sequence, scene management, boot-in-any-scene via [RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod].
  • Balance.cs — all tunable numbers: movement speeds, XP deltas, rating weights, trust thresholds, violence penalty (−25), area gate coordinates.
  • SaveSystem.cs — differential JSON serialisation/deserialisation; autosave on chapter completion, rest, F5, and app-to-background.
  • GameInput.cs — unified keyboard + touch virtual-key model; floating joystick; virtual button presses queued for next-frame consumption.
  • GameEvents.cs — lightweight publish/subscribe event bus replacing Unity's SendMessage overhead.

7.2.2 Story Layer

  • InkLite.cs — 450-line custom Ink parser and runner; all features required by the six chapters implemented and unit-tested.
  • StoryLibrary.cs — loads and concatenates all story TextAssets; resolves Ink INCLUDE directives.
  • StoryState.cs — variable dictionary with typed getters/setters; diff-save support.
  • DialogueManager.cs — implements IStoryHost; binds 12 EXTERNAL functions: has, give, trust, add_trust, joined, chapter, rating, resolve, minigame, unlock, protest, vote, sleep, save.
  • ChoiceTags.cs — parses karma/trust/why/violence tags; shared between runtime and test code.
  • Story Files — all six chapters fully authored in Ink (~2,400 lines total); validated by the story-playthrough EditMode tests.

7.2.3 Core Systems Layer

  • KarmaSystem.cs + KarmaStatAsset.cs — XP accumulation, level computation, rank title lookup, ScriptableObject runtime loading with code-default fallback.
  • TrustSystem.cs — per-resident 0–100 trust, supporter threshold (60+), march-eligibility computation.
  • MissionSystem.cs — chapter detection from Ink doneVar booleans; progress-check hook after every conversation and mini-game.
  • ProtestManager.cs — placard collection, park gathering, four candle-march checkpoints, petition delivery, violence tracking.
  • ElectionManager.cs — stall visits, street play, fact-check with Chacha, door-to-door voter informing, polling booth sequence (ink-on-finger animation, EVM, VVPAT slip).
  • EndingEvaluator.cs — implements the rating formula; selects ending and election winner.
  • VoiceSystem.cs — OS TTS integration (macOS say, Windows Speech Platform); per-character voice; subtitle co-ordination; safely muted when TTS unavailable.

7.2.4 World and AI Layer

  • WorldBuilder.cs — procedural 96 × 64 tilemap; all buildings, streets, park, bazaar, Mela Maidan generated from WorldLayout coordinates.
  • CityVisuals.cs — dynamic reactions: dim facades (<45 rating), broken lights (<40), potholes, graffiti-to-murals (≥60), festival bunting (≥70), repaired park swings (Hero/Jagruk ending).
  • NPC.cs + NPCStates.cs — full FSM: GoTo, Work, Home, Sleep, Talk, Special, Follow; NPC daily schedule; candle-march procession trail using FollowState.
  • Pathfinding/AStar.cs + NavGrid.cs — A* on the tile grid with paved-tile lower-cost preference; tested for full-world reachability.

7.2.5 Automated Test Suite

  • 31 EditMode tests: InkLite unit tests; best-path and worst-path story playthroughs; #why-tag enforcement; map reachability and overlap checks; karma, trust, rating, ending, and mini-game logic validation.

7.3 Independent Game Design

Beyond technical implementation, the team engaged in a substantial independent game design effort that distinguishes Mohalla Hero from a purely software-engineering deliverable.

7.3.1 Narrative Authorship

All story content was independently authored in Ink. The six chapter files represent approximately 2,400 lines of Ink across 12 named characters, 180+ knots, and over 95 distinct player choices. Authoring constraints:

  • At most four visible choices per dialogue page (enforced by an EditMode test).
  • Every choice with |karma| ≥ 5 must carry a #why explanation (enforced by test — currently 47 tags verified).
  • Every persuasion conversation includes a repeatable patient-and-honest option (+5 trust) to prevent soft-locks for players who lied early.
  • World objects (lights, taps, garbage, saplings) use sticky (+) choices so they remain interactable after resolution.

7.3.2 Mini-Game Design

7.3.3 Electoral Sequence Design

The Election Mela (Chapter 6) is the game's most complex level, with nine sequential sub-tasks: visiting three candidate stalls, attending the street play, fact-checking with Chacha Gossip, informing five voters door-to-door (honest vs. pressure branches), and the polling booth sequence (ID verification, ink-on-finger animation, EVM with four candidate rows, VVPAT paper slip confirmation, and the "Vote ho gaya" closure screen). This sequence was designed to demystify the Indian voting process for first-time voters and school students, many of whom have never seen an EVM or VVPAT.

7.3.4 Character and World Design

All twelve resident characters were independently designed with distinct personality profiles, daily schedules (via NPC FSM), dialogue voice indices, and trust trajectories. Key characters: Pintu (meme kid, serves as in-game guide), Chacha Gossip (tea-stall fake-news propagator), Netaji Jugaad Singh and Dr. Vaada Verma (rival candidates), Asha Ma'am (school teacher and Chapter 1 mission giver), Sharma Aunty (Chapter 3 mission giver), Meera Didi (civic activist, Chapters 4–5), and Officer Fernandes (Chapter 6 election officer). The world map — 96 × 64 tiles — was validated for full reachability by automated flood-fill tests covering gate-sealing, overlap-freedom, and A* path preference.

8. UI Interface Design and Screenshots

8.1 Design Philosophy and Colour Palette

The UI design philosophy is "city app" — the interface evokes the smartphone apps that target-audience players use daily: rounded cards, a persistent status bar, and a smartphone-within-the-game (the Mohalla Phone) as the player's mission and information hub. All UI is constructed procedurally by UIFactory.cs using Unity uGUI. The reference resolution is 1920 × 1080, scaled with Scale With Screen Size (match 0.5), ensuring adaptation to phones, tablets, and monitors without redesign. The game is landscape-only; touch controls appear automatically on touch-screen devices.

10. Conclusion

Mohalla Hero: Choices That Count represents a carefully engineered intersection of game-based learning theory, independent narrative game design, and robust software engineering. By the mid-semester evaluation, the project has delivered a fully functional Unity 6 game with a complete six-chapter branching story (~2,400 lines of Ink), four interconnected gameplay systems (karma, trust, mission, and rating), a procedurally generated 96 × 64-tile city world reacting dynamically to player behaviour, three independently designed mini-games, a fully code-generated UI system, a custom Ink interpreter with zero external dependencies, and a comprehensive 32-test automated test suite covering story correctness, world reachability, and all system logic.

The project successfully demonstrates that civic education can be made deeply engaging without sacrificing academic rigour or real-world accuracy. The "Why it Matters" card system, the cross-chapter consequence architecture, the Mohalla Rating formula, and the secret-vote design together create a learning loop that mirrors Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle within a 60–90 minute first playthrough. The fictional but culturally authentic setting — Hinglish dialogue, familiar Indian neighbourhood geography, EVM and VVPAT mechanics, and the RTI as a plot tool — gives the game a credibility and relevance that distinguishes it from generic civic apps.

The remaining six weeks of work — audio, Android testing, accessibility, and final documentation — are well-scoped within the schedule. The code-first architecture ensures the game can be delivered, demonstrated, and distributed without dependency on paid assets, external services, or specialised hardware.

Beyond this semester, the project has clear post-submission potential. The modular story architecture allows additional chapters without engine changes; InkLite is reusable for any Ink-based Unity project; and the civic theme selection maps directly to SVEEP, NCERT, and ELC curricula, making it a credible candidate for adoption by educational NGOs, municipal civic education programmes, and state-level voter literacy initiatives.
